# PotPress market and viability

PotPress is for small filter factories and the NGOs that start them, competing with imported presses at $3,000 to $3,500 and with one-off local builds. An open design matters because the press is the factory's single largest piece of forming equipment and has to be repaired where the factory is. Prices and benchmarks were checked 2026-10-23. Method: STANDARDS section 23.

Table 1. Market segments.

| Segment | Target users and region | Competitive benchmark (cited price) | Estimated cost per unit (USD) | Value proposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing filter factories | Factories in Latin America, Africa and Asia replacing or adding a press | Potters for Peace press, about $3,000 to $3,500 (PPR-PRB-001) | 1,279 in parts plus local labour | About 40 % of the imported cost; repairable with local steel and a bottle jack |
| New factories | NGOs and social enterprises starting production | Nigerian locally built press, about $1,000; Potters Without Borders scissor press, about $2,300 (PPR-PRB-001) | 1,279 | Open drawings, build plan and QC rack in one package |
| Universities and labs | Ceramics and water research groups | Penn State low-cost press, under $200 with a 2 t jack (Henry, Maley and Mehta) | 1,279 | Known, repeatable force and geometry for studies |

The product the press serves sells for about $7.50 to $30 per filter where made locally (CDC, 2008), $8 in Cambodia and $10 to $15 in Nicaragua (Akvopedia), and a factory costs about $15,000 to $30,000 to set up (Akvopedia). At the 69 pots per 6 h on paper, the press's parts cost equals the sale price of about 40 to 170 filters, a few days of output.

## Open route

Local fabrication shops build the frame from the drawings; city foundries cast the molds from the printed patterns; filter networks and NGOs carry the design to new factories. CERN-OHL-S keeps changes open, and the parametric model lets a factory change the filter size without a new design.
